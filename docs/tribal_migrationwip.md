## Tribal migration

Independent territorial tribes and steppe hordes can choose **Become a Migrating People** from **Country Content > Decisions**; the decision selects a valid neighbouring camp automatically. The action in **Form New Country** lets the player choose the camp manually. Both use the same effect and can launch only one total migration per country. The effect gathers all country-owned nobles and soldiers, 60% of burghers (the game's artisan/urban POP type), laborers and clergy, and 20% of peasants and tribesmen in an unowned frontier camp. It raises a special escort and converts the *same country* to EU5's army-based type, so its army can be commanded normally. The country keeps its identity, culture and religion. The former locations are abandoned; the camp stays as a territorial anchor.

After moving the escort onto unowned land, choose **Colonize the Land Beneath the Army** in **Country Content > Decisions**. It claims that location and immediately transfers 20% of the camp's native POPs while the country remains army-based. **Settle the Migrating People Permanently**, in the same decision menu, transfers the remaining camp POPs and turns the country territorial again on unowned land or land it already owns. **Move Migrating Population to Army** remains an optional action that schedules a six-month native POP migration after an army move. A separate **Send a Migrating Offshoot** action detaches 10–40% of POPs from one location into an army-based vassal, leaving the parent country intact. The player can choose to switch control to that offshoot.

Limits: both departures require peace, enough population and gold; total migration is once per country, offshoot creation has a 20-year cooldown and a one-active-offshoot limit, and moving a camp has a one-year cooldown. Army-country colonization costs 25 gold, requires peace, has a five-year cooldown and stops at five owned locations including the original camp. Quarterly checks can cause a food shortage or a one-time foreign-camp passage event. See [TECHNICAL_AUDIT.md](TECHNICAL_AUDIT.md), [KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md](KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md), and [HISTORICAL_MIGRATION_ZONES.md](HISTORICAL_MIGRATION_ZONES.md). Vanilla colonization and POP-country migration scripts are untouched.

## Countries, armies, movement, names and interfaces

- Country templates were checked under `main_menu/setup/templates`, including `eurasian_horde_no_muslim.txt`; this mod also defines Bronze Era templates there. Political eligibility follows native government **types**, not template names or country tags.
- `a_tribesmen` is a native unit type adapted by this mod in `in_game/common/unit_types/0_tribal.txt`. `be_migration_escort` copies it and sets `is_special = yes`, `buildable = no`, `levy = no`. Whether the override combination behaves as intended needs a runtime test.
- The native `migrate_pop_based_country` action in `in_game/common/generic_actions/pop_based_countries.txt` handles neighbour-province movement for **POP-based** countries with `add_migration`. This prototype does not replace it.
- The native `settle_country` action converts a POP-based country to a territorial one after advances. Creating a new political group from a partial migration without early independence is not demonstrated by the inspected samples.
- The army is an ordinary controllable EU5 army owned by the army-based country. The unique escort type identifies it in action selectors. A partial departure offers a `change_player` choice to command its new country. The POPs do not live inside the unit object: the country stores a camp location and native `add_migration` is ordered after the army moves. The one-year follow-order cooldown allows the six-month transfer to finish before another order. Direct automatic POP-on-unit movement remains unverified and is not claimed.
- Native scripted country names exist in `in_game/common/scripted_country_names/00_default.txt`; dynamic names for the proposed partial groups have not been audited enough to implement safely.
- Native generic action selectors provide the camp, source, army and population-share choices. The installed game provides `common/decisions` with `government_decisions` and option effects. The migration decision chooses a valid neighbour camp automatically and calls the same scripted effect as the manual generic action. It uses the shared `be_migration_total_used` guard after success so a failed click does not consume an engine-level one-time decision. The existing Form New Country GUI override was rebased to the current game version so its Country Content tabs, button visibility binding and candidate list remain present.

## Army-country colonization decisions (7 November 2026)

The migration escort can now found a colony from **Country Content > Decisions** by standing on an unowned, ownable land location. The country remains `country_type = army`. A separate decision on the same menu permanently settles the escort's location and changes the country to `country_type = location`. Both decisions use native country `any_unit`/`random_unit`, `unit_location`, `change_location_owner`, `change_location_controller`, `change_integration_level` and the native scripted `transfer_pop` effect. The owning country and the selected unit are checked again in the effect. The colonization decision moves 20% of each native camp POP after ownership changes, charges 25 gold, creates a five-year expiring country variable, and requires fewer than five owned locations. Settlement transfers the remaining camp POPs immediately. The old camp remains owned. This design does not alter vanilla colonization files. The new decisions have passed only static validation; their visibility and effect execution require an in-game test.

Departure actions are guarded by the native tribal/horde trigger in `potential`, `allow` and the effect. POP proportions are handled by the native `transfer_pop` scripted effect. The total migration keeps the original country identity; the offshoot is a vassal army-country. # Tribal Migration — known limits and game checks

1. **Army control:** total migration changes the original country to native `country_type = army` and raises special escort sub-units. The player commands the resulting army normally. A partial offshoot is a native vassal army-country; the player chooses whether to switch to that group or remain with the parent country. Its later independence uses normal vassal mechanics. The `change_player` choice still needs a game test.
2. **POP shares:** the initial total migration immediately moves 100% of nobles and soldiers, 60% of burghers (the EU5 artisan/urban category), laborers and clergy, and 20% of peasants and tribesmen from each original country-owned POP into a newly owned camp. Slaves are left behind. The offshoot action moves a player-selected 10–40% of eligible POPs from one source location. The native scripted `transfer_pop` preserves type, culture and religion; POP conservation needs a game check.
3. **POP movement after departure:** armies move through the ordinary game controls. The player then uses **Move Migrating Population to Army**, which schedules a six-month native `add_migration` from the recorded camp to the army's location. This is a separate order, so the POPs are not literally stored in the army and can lag behind it. The one-year cooldown prevents repeated orders within that six-month period. Whether `to_owner` retains migrant POP ownership in foreign locations must be checked in-game.
4. **Land, colonization and settlement:** a total migration abandons its former locations but keeps the newly founded frontier camp. In **Country Content > Decisions**, move the escort onto unowned land and choose **Colonize the Land Beneath the Army** to claim it without ending army-country status. The decision transfers 20% of each camp POP immediately, costs 25 gold, has a five-year cooldown and allows at most four colonies beyond the camp. **Settle the Migrating People Permanently** accepts unowned land or land already owned by the migrant country, moves camp POPs immediately and changes the country back to territorial. Foreign-owned land must be acquired through normal war, peace or diplomatic game mechanics first. The old camp remains owned after settlement; automatic release of an empty camp is not implemented. The new decisions require an escort army in a qualifying location and have not yet been tested in-game.
5. **AI and history:** an AI tribe or horde considers total migration only at low tribal cohesion or horde unity, every 36 months, with a food preference for the next camp. AI offshoots can use the follow order. This is a generic fallback, not the requested people/date-based historical destination database. `HISTORICAL_MIGRATION_ZONES.md` explains why example people were not hardcoded. The normal EU5 army AI decides subsequent movement; no verified script effect was found to order an AI army along a historical route.
6. **Passage and conflict:** the game handles army access and combat normally. A quarterly check raises one foreign-camp passage event after the player orders POPs to follow into another country's location; payment costs 20 gold, while refusal reduces cohesion or horde unity. It does not grant military access or transfer payment to the foreign owner. Direct diplomatic negotiations, tribute, illness, political rupture events and special hostile settlement are not implemented. The other quarterly camp event handles severe food shortages with either a purchase or native POP/cohesion loss.
7. **Visual and names:** the escort uses existing light-unit graphics. The original country retains its name; offshoot naming is generated by the engine. There is no custom icon, population counter on the army, historical naming database or custom GUI.
8. **Runtime validation:** the user launched the previous build, and its logs showed an invalid `province_capital` scope from `province_definition`, which stopped the migration effect. This was corrected, along with event AI weights and an offshoot player-switch scope, but the corrected build has not yet been launched. The especially uncertain points remain `create_army_country_in_location` with the vassal reform, `change_country_type` while retaining the camp, `abandon_location` after moving POPs, and foreign-camp `add_migration` ownership. No save compatibility or multi-mod compatibility claim is made.
9. **Country Content and GUI:** the decision selects an eligible neighbouring camp automatically; the Form New Country action offers manual selection. Both share the departure effect and the permanent one-total-migration-per-country guard. The Form New Country override now follows the installed game's current layout and restores village-to-clan and clan-to-tribe buttons; visual placement and click behaviour need an in-game check.

## Suggested in-game acceptance sequence

Start a new Bronze Era campaign. For a territorial tribe and a steppe horde, record POP sizes in all source locations, use **Become a Migrating People**, then verify the seven category shares in the camp and the abandoned land. Command the escort into a neighbouring location, use **Move Migrating Population to Army**, wait six months, and inspect POP owner, culture, religion and size. If the destination is foreign-owned, verify the passage event occurs once. Settle on unowned land; save and reload. In separate saves, create an offshoot with each play choice and confirm the parent country persists, the new country is a vassal, and its army can move when played. Confirm a monarchy cannot see either departure action and that vanilla colonization and POP-country migration still work. Inspect fresh `error.log` for invalid effects, scopes, reform, unit type and localization keys.

# Historical migration zones — data plan

The current build uses a generic cohesion/unity crisis and neighbouring-camp food score for AI departure. It has no people-specific historical zones. The peoples and destinations in the design request are illustrative; none are hardcoded. No province, region or culture identifiers are guessed.

The Bronze Era campaign uses an internal positive-year calendar. For example, internal year 1 displays 1209 BC and internal year 1063 displays 147 BC; see `docs/bronze_era_positive_chronology.md`. Future data must fit the mod's playable chronology and use exact identifiers found in its map and culture definitions.

Proposed later schema: zone ID, localized name, valid culture or people keys, internal start/end dates, preferred and discouraged region/province IDs, date-dependent weight, activation conditions, and a fallback weight for military danger, food and distance. Preference weights must remain soft so a dangerous historical target can be bypassed. EU5 has a native `in_game/common/area_preferences` data category for conquest and exploration, but no inspected native category for migration destinations. A later version must map zone data into verified action scoring or another supported category; a custom `common/tribal_migration/historical_migration_zones` folder would not by itself be parsed by the game.
